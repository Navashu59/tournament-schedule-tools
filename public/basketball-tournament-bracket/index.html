Court-first bracket planning

Basketball brackets are constrained by court availability. One court creates a simple but longer event. Two courts can move the opening round quickly, but later rounds may still collapse to one featured court.

Set match length from the whole gym block, not only game time. Warmups, halftime, scorekeeper setup, overtime rules, and team changeover all affect the published schedule.

Bracket setup

  1. Enter teams in seed order if rankings matter.
  2. Choose single elimination for a short event or double elimination when one loss should not end the day.
  3. Set available courts and first tipoff time.
  4. Generate the bracket and scan the first two rounds.
  5. Print or export after checking byes and gym timing.

Basketball-specific checks

  • Decide whether higher seeds receive byes when the field is not a power of two.
  • Confirm whether third-place or consolation games are expected.
  • Add rest time before the final if teams may play back-to-back.
  • Put score reporting rules on the printed bracket or event message.
  • Rename courts to Gym A, Main Court, or the exact venue language.

How do I schedule a basketball bracket across limited courts?

Assign each gym court as a venue and use realistic time blocks. If only one court is available, publish exact start times so teams are not waiting through the whole bracket.

Should a basketball tournament include a consolation game?

Add a third-place or consolation game when teams travel for the event and expect more than one guaranteed game.
